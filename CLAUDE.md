# Express Photo and Gifts

E-commerce website for a photo printing and gift shop. See `PRODUCT.md` for users, positioning and stack decisions, and `DESIGN.md` for the design system (colours, type, components).

## Current scope

- The homepage and the shop exist: `/shop` (storefront) and `/shop/<slug>` (catalogue for `all`, `specials` or a category, with rail, type chips, occasion and recipient filters, sort, price band and photo filter). Product pages exist at `/shop/product/<slug>` (`app/shop/product/`), where Add to cart fills the cart (the page's only order route; WhatsApp there is only for an unpriced variant or asking about a date) and a photo-needs block shows the photo count, print area and shape from `photoSpec` before the customer commits. The cart is client-side (`app/site/cartStore.ts`, localStorage; line logic in `lib/catalog/cart.ts`): once it holds anything, the dock's gold pill becomes Cart with the unit count and opens the cart drawer (`app/site/Cart.tsx`), which the header's cart mark (`HeaderCart`, with the count on a gold disc) opens at any count, which re-checks prices against `/api/prices` and goes on to checkout: the photo step, or straight to details when nothing takes a photo. The photo step of checkout exists at `/checkout` (`app/checkout/`): every cart line that takes photos is drawn as its slots (count, print area and shape from `photoSpec`), beside one tray of uploads; a photo can fill any number of slots, and each slot has its own edit (crop, zoom, quarter turns, greyscale, a print turned on its side; `lib/catalog/checkout.ts`), and nothing judges a photo's sharpness (removed on purpose: that is the shop's to check); zooming out past filling shows the whole photo with white borders. Frames say the print is not included and offer the matching print (here and in the cart drawer, via `/api/needs`). Uploads go from the browser to Cloudinary under `orders/<ref>/`, signed by `/api/uploads/sign` one file per signature (the signature names the file) and rate-limited per IP (`lib/rateLimit.ts`). The photo step makes the ref with a secret key kept in localStorage; the first order saved under a ref claims it with the key's hash, and only that key can replace the unpaid order. An unpaid checkout starts over in the browser after 10 days, and a daily cron (`vercel.json` → `/api/cron/sweep-uploads`, `lib/checkout/uploads.ts`, which also sweeps expired email sign-up links) deletes the photos of orders never paid after 14, and those of paid orders on no account (`uid` null) a year after payment (`sweepGuestPhotos`, `GUEST_PHOTO_DAYS`; `settings/photoSweep.through` marks the payment time swept to, moved on to the earliest order that failed, if any; an order that joins an account first keeps its photos). The photos of an order on an account are never swept: they stay until the customer deletes them under Your orders (`deletePhotos` in `lib/account/orders.ts`, refused while the order is on its way) or deletes the account; a photo another of the account's orders still places by Order again is kept until that order's photos go too (`deleteOrderPhotos`' `keep`). Either way the order stays as the sales record with its line records and a `photosDeletedAt`, which hides the photos in the account, the receipt and the admin's print sheet (`holdsPhotos` in `lib/checkout/order.ts`), and leaves Order again with empty places A photo is placed whole and untouched by default (never auto-cropped), and an untouched place sends the original upload; a place the customer edits is rendered in the browser at full resolution and uploaded as its own file (`bake` in `app/checkout/images.ts`). The photo step goes on to `/checkout/details` (`app/checkout/details/`): contact, collect-or-courier (The Courier Guy, `lib/checkout/courier.ts`: quoted for the parcels the cart packs into (`lib/checkout/packing.ts`: each line's size and weight times its quantity, counted into rows, columns and layers of the smallest of the three boxes in `lib/checkout/boxes.ts` that holds it, more than one parcel when it doesn't fit, own packaging at its own size when no box does, the standard box at least when something has no measurements), the boxes' names, outside sizes and empty weights set in the admin's Settings (`settings/shop.boxes`, placeholders until saved), and each product's `shipping` (length, width, height, weight, packed; `lib/catalog/measures.ts`, overridable per variant), set in the editor's Checkout tab, where a measure worked out from the description and photos rather than measured is marked Estimated until staff type or confirm it; the order's value is declared up to the free cover, R1,000, and above it the customer may tick to insure the full value, capped at TCG's R30,000; an optional gift recipient's name and mobile become TCG's delivery contact), a review with the placed photos, and payment by PayFast (`lib/checkout/payfast.ts`: Onsite window with the redirect form as fallback). Placing an order (`placeOrder`) re-prices everything on the server (orders under R5, PayFast's floor, are refused), writes `orders/{ref}` as pending and freezes it as a payment attempt (`orders/{ref}/attempts/{ref}-{n}`, PayFast's `m_payment_id`), so a payment always pays for exactly what was on screen when Pay was pressed (`lib/checkout/orders.ts`). Only the PayFast notice at `/api/payfast/notify`, after its four checks (signature with the required passphrase, PayFast's published IP ranges or hosts, amount to the cent against its attempt, and PayFast's validate), marks it paid (when PayFast's Onsite window, or its return to the receipt, says the payment went through, `reportPaid` stamps the attempt as `reportedPaid`, which marks nothing paid but stops `startAttempt` taking a second payment for two days, `awaitingNotice`). Every notice is recorded under `orders/{ref}/notices`; a second payment, a wrong amount or a signed notice that failed a check flags the order (`flagged`, `issues`) for the shop, and an unsigned one is never written. Once paid, the shop and the customer are emailed through Resend (`lib/checkout/paid.ts`, `lib/checkout/emails.ts`, `lib/email.ts`). A "Save my details for next time" tick under the contact fields adds a password field beside the email; paying then makes the account (before the pending order is saved, so the order carries it) and signs the customer in. The receipt is `/checkout/done?ref=&t=`, which every customer email links to as "Track my order": it tracks the order as one line of steps, the shop's stages (timed by `stagedAt`) joined to The Courier Guy's events once booked (`lib/checkout/tracking.ts`), redraws every minute while open, and asks TCG again after the page is sent when the stored status is over 10 minutes old (once per order per window). When the order has no account, it offers one (`lib/account/auth.ts`, session helpers shared with the admin in `lib/identity.ts`); `/account` (`app/account/`, `lib/account/orders.ts`) signs in; signed in, it tracks every order still on its way with the receipt's tracking card, lists every paid order on the account (`orders/{ref}.uid`) by the customer's own placed photos with its receipt, Order again and Delete photos (the shop says it keeps them until they do), and holds the saved details (edited in place), one marketing switch (`marketing/{uid}`: the first agreement's date kept, every change after it added to `changes`), Change password (other devices signed out) and Delete account (password to confirm; refused while an order is on its way; deletes the sign-in, `customers/{uid}`, `marketing/{uid}` and every order's photos, and keeps the orders unlinked as sales records). Order again puts the same lines in the cart at today's prices and stock, with the old placed files back in the photo step (`photos.reuse`); `placeOrder` accepts a file from outside the order's folder only when it was placed on a paid order on the same account (`reusableFiles`). Orders placed with the email before the account join it only once a link emailed to that address proves it (`/account/verify`, a hashed one-day token in `customers/{uid}.emailCheck`); after that, guest orders with the email join on each visit. Checkout no longer sends orders on WhatsApp. Settings live in `.env.local`: `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_KEY`, `FIREBASE_WEB_API_KEY`, `ADMIN_EMAILS`, `SITE_URL`, `PAYFAST_MERCHANT_ID`, `PAYFAST_MERCHANT_KEY`, `PAYFAST_PASSPHRASE`, `PAYFAST_SANDBOX`, `PAYFAST_ONSITE`, `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM`, `ORDER_EMAIL_TO` (only the default until Settings saves who the shop's new-order email goes to, `settings/shop`, `lib/settings.ts`; the same Shop Contact Details section sets the public email and mobile number, `settings/shop.contact`, read through `shopContact()` and `lib/contact.ts` by every page, email, the receipt PDF, PayFast's confirmation address and TCG's shop contact, and by client components through `useContact`/`useWhatsApp` in `app/site/ContactContext.tsx`; `SHOP` in `app/site/data.ts` only holds the starting values), `TCG_API_KEY`, `TCG_API_URL` (production, `https://api.portal.thecourierguy.co.za`, when unset; the sandbox is `https://api.shiplogic.com` with a sandbox account's key), `TCG_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `CRON_SECRET` (the crons refuse to run without it), `VERCEL_API_TOKEN`, `VERCEL_PROJECT_ID` (set by Vercel on its own deployments), `VERCEL_TEAM_ID` (the Analytics page's traffic).
- Stock is per variant (SKU), in `stock/{sku}` with every change under `moves` (`lib/stock.ts`; the rules shared with the browser in `lib/catalog/stock.ts`). A SKU is untracked, and always orderable, until staff switch it on at `/admin/stock` (`app/admin/(console)/stock/`, `lib/admin/stock.ts`: receive a delivery, set a count, which is refused if the count moved since the page loaded, and set Sold out at, where the size shows Sold out on the shop, and Low at, which warns). Two caches, so a sale doesn't re-render the shop: pages read only which SKUs are sold out (`getSoldOut`, tag `stock-sold-out`, cleared only when a SKU goes in or out of sold out) for Sold out sizes, cards and schema.org availability, and the counts (`getAvailability`, tag `stock`) reach the browser only through `/api/stock`, which the cart and the product page fetch; asking for more than there is shows the most available now with "Order N" or "Ask on WhatsApp" (`app/site/StockLimit.tsx`). Every count change goes through `writeLevel` and clears the caches itself (`stockChanged`). `placeOrder` checks the live counts; paying takes the units off inside `applyNotice`'s transaction (kept on the order as `stock`), flags an oversold order, and emails the new-order recipients once when a SKU first reaches Low (`lowStockEmail`). A paid order's page has Put back in stock (`returnStock`, once), for a refund or cancellation however it was made. The dashboard has a Running low card.
- Campaigns (`/admin/campaigns`, `app/admin/campaigns/`, storage `lib/admin/campaigns.ts`) are the shop's deals: each holds one or more rules written as sentences (price off: percent, rand or a sale price; buy X get Y; buy this and that is reduced; spend a threshold and save), aimed at chosen products and sizes, categories, tags or everything, with dates in SAST, caps (orders, units, and Needs a one-time code, below), a storefront feature and a log. One engine, `lib/catalog/campaigns.ts`, prices them in the browser and on the server: everything stacks, price deals first (the price shown on the shop), then item deals, then spend thresholds on what is left, never below R0. Cart lines keep the catalogue price; `/api/deals` (static, five minutes) gives the cart the live and soon-starting campaigns, whose dates the browser checks itself; `placeOrder` reads them fresh (`lib/campaigns.ts`), each order line keeps its catalogue `base` price and, when item deals or a spend threshold cut it further, `paid` (what refunding that line alone gives back; the admin's print sheet shows it), and the order its `discount` and `savings`; the engine breaks ties by SKU, never cart order, so browser and server agree, and when they still differ `placeOrder` returns its own total, which checkout shows and the next Pay pays, and paying counts each campaign's use in the same transaction. The Specials shelf, the card and product-page deal words and the storefront's specials band all come from live campaigns (the old hand-set `special` flag is gone; products still carrying it are offered as a draft campaign). Shop pages rebuild every five minutes so starts and ends land on time. The editor warns, never blocks (`campaignWarnings`), on deals that make something free or take over half off, a sale price that changes nothing, a spend saving over half the spend, shop wording naming amounts the deal doesn't give, sold-out and low stock on either side of a deal, a unit cap above stock, and stacked prices reaching R0 or under PayFast's floor, and a code-only campaign marked to feature; the low-stock email and the dashboard name the live campaign selling a low size.
- One-time codes (`caps.code`, "Needs a one-time code" in the campaign editor; `lib/campaignCodes.ts`, the shape in `lib/catalog/codes.ts`) replace the old once-per-customer cap, which is gone (a stored `perCustomer` is ignored). A campaign that needs a code is kept off the shop altogether: `getOnCampaigns` drops it, so it is on no shelf, card, product page, the storefront band, `/api/deals` or `/llms.txt`, and `placeOrder` reads only the other live ones fresh (`readLiveDeals`). Email all with that campaign chosen makes each customer their own code before anything is sent (`issueCodes`: 10 characters of an alphabet without 0 O 1 I, worked out as an HMAC of the mailing's id and the address, keyed from the service-account key, so a retried send makes the same codes and therefore the same emails; kept in `campaignCodes/{code}` with the campaign, the mailing and `to`, the hash of who it was sent to, never the address). The email shows it large and says to enter it in the Discount code box at checkout and that it works once, on one order, made for that email (`lib/campaignEmail.ts`; also in the plain text); the preview shows `SAMPLE-CODE`, and Send me a test makes a real code for the staff member (`test: true`) so checkout can be tried. A code isn't tied to an address. Checkout's "Have a discount code?" (`app/checkout/details/CodeBox.tsx`, in the review step) calls `checkCodeAction` (rate-limited per IP), which says why a code can't be used (unknown, used, starts on a date, ended, paused, no longer needed) or sends back the campaign as the engine takes it; the cart keeps the code and that campaign in `epg:code:v1` beside the lines (`cart.applyCode`/`removeCode`; cleared with the cart once paid) and prices them stacked in the same order the server uses (`dealsNow`), the details step asks the server about a kept code once when it opens, and delivery quotes count the code's deals in the goods' value. `placeOrder` checks the code again (`dealsForOrder`); one that can't be used is refused with `field: "code"` and the browser takes it off, and one whose deal saves nothing on this cart is left off the order so it isn't spent. The code is frozen on the payment attempt like the savings (`Snapshot.code`) and copied to the order; it is used up only inside `applyNotice`'s transaction (`redeemCode`, read before any write), so an abandoned or failed payment costs nothing and a code is never held for an order that isn't paid. Two orders paid with one code at once (it can't be prevented without holding the code, which would lock a customer out of their own after a closed PayFast window) both stand: the second is flagged for the shop. Refunds don't return a code. The campaign page shows codes emailed (the sum of `mailings.codes`) against codes used (its paid orders); the order's Money card shows the code.
- The admin's Customers page (`/admin/customers`, `app/admin/(console)/customers/`, data in `lib/admin/customers.ts`) is read-only and read fresh: one customer per email, every paid order joined under it (an order placed signed in counts under its account's email) with the account, if any; accounts with no order, and email-only subscribers (the footer's "Email me specials", below), show only under Marketing email when they agreed. Views All, Regulars (2+ paid orders, by spend) and Marketing email, whose opted-in list downloads as CSV (`/admin/customers/export`). A customer's page (`/admin/customers/<key>`, the `customerKey` email hash, so no email in the URL; any other email their paid orders used finds them too) shows contact (call, WhatsApp, email), every order including unpaid ones, what they buy, deals used, courier addresses and gift recipients, and the marketing consent history (`allConsent` in `lib/account/auth.ts`). A paid order's glance card links to its customer. Email all (`EmailAll.tsx`, `lib/admin/mailings.ts`) writes one subject and message to every opted-in customer (an account's switch or an email-only subscription, merged by email), read fresh when Send is pressed: each customer gets their own email (`marketingEmail`, sent in Resend batches of 100 by `sendBatch`, keyed by an id made when the dialog opens so a repeat can't email anyone twice) saying why they got it, with a one-tap Unsubscribe link and the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (RFC 8058; `lib/unsubscribe.ts` signs the link for the address, so it needs no sign-in) and a link to the account; a campaign may be picked (live or scheduled, from the page's `emailableCampaigns`; one that needs a code gets each customer their own, and the dialog says which case applies), and the server then builds its block from the campaign as it stands when the email is made (`buildFeature` in `lib/campaignFeature.ts`, rendered by `lib/campaignEmail.ts`: each rule as a shopper sentence, when it runs in SAST, its once-per-customer and order or unit caps, and up to six photo tiles of the products the shop's own cards would call specials, sold out ones left out, a spend over everything showing only its sentence; a code-only campaign's links go to the whole shop, not the Specials shelf, since its deal isn't there; a draft, paused or ended campaign is refused), with a Preview of the email as customers get it; Send me a test goes to the signed-in staff member first; every send is kept under `mailings/{id}` with who sent it, what, which campaign and how many.

once delivered, its proof of delivery (read from TCG on request; the links expire) and a return to the shop (`returnShipment`, reference `<ref>-R`), handled by the same functions with a direction; the order is held while TCG is asked, and a booking whose outcome is unknown must be looked up, by the order's reference among TCG's recent shipments, before booking again, since TCG has no idempotency key; then the waybill and stickers PDFs, cancel, and its status, kept by TCG's unsigned webhook at `/api/tcg/webhook?key=` (which only triggers a re-read from TCG) and a daily cron, `/api/cron/track-shipments`; the order moves to Sent once TCG has it, problems are flagged, and the customer is emailed the tracking number only when the courier has collected, `syncShipment`, not when it is booked), refunds (`lib/checkout/refunds.ts` works out the amount by the terms: the reason, the units at what was paid for them after deals, delivery only where the terms give it back, and anything the terms let the shop keep, with its reason; `lib/admin/refunds.ts` makes it through PayFast's API to the card paid with, holding the order as `refunding` while PayFast is asked and settling an unclear answer by PayFast's refunded total, never a blind retry, or records one made in PayFast's dashboard (an EFT's) once PayFast's record shows it; the customer is emailed what was refunded and what was kept; a refund of everything before it left the shop cancels the order: stage `cancelled`, which only a refund sets, and a waybill still standing is flagged; customers cancel by contacting the shop (no self-service), and staff use Cancel the order on the Payment tab (`cancelOrder`: the customer asked, 10.2/10.3, the shop can't supply it, 10.4, which needs a message telling the customer why, or late, 10.5; everything still due is refunded, less only what 10.2 lets the shop keep for personalised work already started, then a waybill the courier hasn't collected is cancelled and, when ticked, the stock put back; the customer's email is worded by the reason and carries the message)), issues to mark as looked at, and its emails to resend, with every change logged by who made it. Payments (`/admin/payments`, `lib/admin/payments.ts`) sets PayFast's own transaction history for a month beside the orders (read live through PayFast's signed API, `lib/checkout/payfastApi.ts`), exceptions first: a payment whose notice never arrived, a wrong amount, a second payment, one naming no order, or a paid order PayFast has no record of. A daily cron (`/api/cron/payfast-check`, 06:00 SAST, first of the three crons) and Check now flag each exception on its order once (`orders/{ref}/notices/history-…`), and also a refund PayFast made that the order doesn't record and an attempt PayFast's window reported paid with no payment in its history; the check reads back to the day before the last one that succeeded (`meta/payfastCheck.covered`, up to 45 days), and the upload sweep deletes the photos of an order Pay was pressed on only once that coverage has passed its last day; a wrong-amount, second, no-order or not-in-PayFast line is settled by Mark as looked at on its row (a note, kept under `paymentReviews/{key}` with who and when; `reviewException` in `lib/admin/payments.ts`), after which it stops counting on the page and the dashboard (`recountCheck` redraws the last check's count; a missed payment can't be marked, only accepted, and an order's own issues are still resolved on the order); an unpaid order whose payment PayFast's history shows can be accepted from its page (the only way the admin marks an order paid, through the same path as a notice). An order's payment panel also asks PayFast about its card payment and reads its refunds from PayFast; the dashboard shows the last check and PayFast's status page when it reports trouble. Products is a table with bulk status and special changes, and an editor (`app/admin/editor/`) with tabs for details, prices, photos, customer photo and filters, a live preview, a save bar, a leave guard and Duplicate. Every write is a server action in `app/admin/actions.ts` that checks the session itself; storage is `lib/admin/products.ts` (reads fresh, not cached) and the rules shared with the browser are `lib/catalog/edit.ts`. Saving refreshes the shop through the `catalog` cache tag. Product photos upload from the browser to Cloudinary, signed by `lib/admin/cloudinary.ts`, and photos staff remove are destroyed there.

## Stack

- Next.js 16 (App Router, React 19), TypeScript. Read the bundled Next docs before writing code (see AGENTS.md).
- Mantine v9 is the primary UI library, with Tailwind v4 alongside it for layout. Mantine docs: https://mantine.dev/llms.txt
- GSAP for motion.
- Firebase (Firestore) holds the product catalogue and orders; Firebase Auth signs in staff and customers (server side only). Payments go through PayFast.
- `package.json` pins `jwks-rsa` to 3.2.2 through `overrides`. `firebase-admin` 14 asks for `jwks-rsa` 4, whose `jose` 6 is ESM-only and loaded with `require()`; Vercel's Node 24 runtime refuses that (`ERR_REQUIRE_ESM`), so every page that loads `firebase-admin/auth` fails with a 500 there, though it works locally. `jwks-rsa` 3.2.2 uses `jose` 4, which ships CommonJS, and `firebase-admin` uses only its unchanged client (for App Check and phone-number tokens; sessions don't touch it). The override wins over whatever a newer `firebase-admin` asks for: when upgrading it, check what it needs, and remove the override only once loading works with `node --no-experimental-require-module node_modules/next/dist/bin/next start` (which reproduces Vercel's behaviour).

## Catalogue (Firebase)

- Firestore project `expressphoto-c94ab`. Schema and its conventions live in `lib/catalog/types.ts`: `categories/{id}` and `products/{slug}` with embedded options, variants (SKU + price in ZAR cents) and media (`role: "photo" | "dimension"` — dimension diagrams appear only on the detailed product page).
- Five categories (`photo-gifts`, `frames`, `photo-printing`, `jewellery`, `add-ons`); finer grouping is tags, not sub-categories. Tags are `facet:value` from the closed vocabulary in `lib/catalog/tags.ts`: `type`, `occasion` and `for` are shopper filters (in the URL as `?type=&occasion=&for=`); `process`, `fits` and `holds` are internal, and a `fits` meeting a `holds` is a "Goes with this" pairing (`lib/catalog/related.ts`). Old category URLs redirect in `next.config.ts`.
- The admin console is where the catalogue is edited, including what the customer supplies (`personalisation`) and tags; specials are campaigns. `scripts/catalog-merchandising.mts` only seeds personalisation and tags for the importer, which fails on a product missing from it and never writes `special`.
- Read it only on the server through `lib/catalog/queries.ts` (Admin SDK, cached under the `catalog` tag). `firestore.rules` denies all browser access; deploy changes with `firebase deploy --only firestore:rules`.
- `npm run import:catalog` seeds Firestore from `Express Photo Website Catalogue.xlsx` and refuses to write over a catalogue that already has products; `-- --replace` replaces it, discarding every admin edit, and `-- --dry-run` writes `catalog-preview.json` instead. Prices come from the "Product Catalogue" sheet, text from the "Quick List" sheet; products without a price import as `draft` and are not shown. Print-process prefixes ("Sublimation") are stripped from names and kept in `tags`.

## Images (Cloudinary)

- Product photos live in Cloudinary under `products/<code>`, not in `public/`. Render them with `ProductImage` (`app/site/ProductImage.tsx`), which uses `next-cloudinary` with a fixed set of widths.
- Credentials are in `.env.local` (`NEXT_PUBLIC_CLOUDINARY_CLOUD_NAME`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_CLOUDINARY_API_KEY`, `CLOUDINARY_API_SECRET`). Never expose the secret to client code.
- Cloudinary is on the free tier and must stay there. Keep storage, bandwidth and transformation usage to a minimum: store assets no larger than they are displayed, deliver with f_auto/q_auto at the smallest width that renders sharply, and limit the number of distinct derived sizes (add to the shared width list in `ProductImage` rather than inventing new ones).

## Mobile text size

Text smaller than 16px on form inputs, textareas and select menus makes mobile Safari zoom in when tapped. No text element may be smaller than 16px on mobile.
